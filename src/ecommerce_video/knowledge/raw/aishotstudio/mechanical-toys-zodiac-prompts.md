## Transition Prompt
A smartphone video filmed handheld with one hand from an elevated angle — roughly 45 to 60 degrees above the desk, looking down and slightly forward, as if a person is standing over the table filming with one hand while the other hand interacts with the toy. The desk surface fills most of the frame. The camera has natural human imperfections — subtle shaky movements, slight tilts, minor focus hunting, small overcorrections as the person adjusts their grip on the phone. The footage feels completely authentic, like someone excitedly recording with one hand on their phone while using their free hand to interact with the toy. The video opens on the toy as shown in the start frame, resting on the desk in its compact cylinder form. The camera drifts slightly then corrects — natural handheld sway. A hand enters the frame from the bottom edge — the person's free hand reaching over the desk. The camera jiggles slightly as their body shifts to reach forward. The hand picks up the compact cylinder carefully, lifting it just an inch or two off the desk while keeping it in the same orientation it was resting in — no flipping, no rotating, no tilting. The thumb finds the silver button on top and presses it down with a satisfying tactile click. A brief pause — nothing happens yet. The hand sets the toy back down in the EXACT same spot and EXACT same orientation it picked it up from, then pulls away out of frame. The camera sways gently as the person shifts their weight back. The toy sits motionless on the desk for a half-second beat of anticipation. A faint internal whirring begins — barely audible, like tiny gears engaging. CRITICAL — TRANSFORMATION MUST BE 100% MECHANICAL. NO morphing. NO fading. NO dissolving. NO crossfades. NO parts appearing or disappearing magically. NO smooth shape-blending. Every visible change must be a discrete physical movement: a panel hinging open, a segment sliding out from inside another, a leg unfolding from a recessed compartment, a part rotating on a visible pivot. Every feature in the final deployed form must trace back to a specific seam, panel, hinge, or compartment that was visible on the compact form — nothing materializes from nowhere. The toy stays in place on the desk throughout — it expands outward into its final form within its existing footprint. It does not jump, lift, roll, or reorient itself. The transformation begins. The midsection panel line wrapping around the cylinder cracks open with a sharp metallic click, splitting the cylinder cleanly into a front goat half and a rear fish half that remain connected by an internal articulated spine. The two top panels of the front goat half lift slightly on twin spine hinges along the back, revealing the folded goat body packed inside. The front silver chrome end cap splits along a small vertical seam and hinges open to reveal the goat's snout, which extends forward on a short telescoping neck mechanism with a ratcheting tick-tick-tick, the lower jaw dropping on a pivot and a small tufted beard panel flicking down from a chin slot with a tiny spring snap. From the top of the head, two coiled horn segments emerge from concealed slots flanking the skull, each horn spiraling backward and curling in a tight ridged coil through a series of ratcheting clicks as nested segments telescope out one at a time, locking into their final spiral with a crisp metallic ping. Two front legs deploy from clearly recessed leg compartments along the underside of the front half — the front-left leg swinging down on its shoulder ball joint with the lower segment snapping straight and the cloven hoof making a light metallic tap on the desk, then the front-right leg mirroring the same motion with another tap. The two hinged back panels that had lifted earlier now rotate outward and fold down along the flanks on side hinges, settling into place as the goat's shoulder armor plates with a deep satisfying thunk. A series of small textured fur panels fan out across the chest and upper back from beneath the now-settled shoulder plates — each one rotating into place on tiny hinges with soft servo whirrs. Now the rear fish half begins its deployment. The rear silver chrome end cap splits along a top seam and twin tail-fluke fins fan out and unfurl on radial pivots with a soft pneumatic hiss, spreading wide. The rear half of the cylinder elongates as segmented scaled body sections slide backward one by one along an internal flex-spine with ratcheting tick-tick-tick, each segment telescoping out from inside the one ahead of it. As the segments deploy, the entire rear body curves sideways and upward in a graceful S-curve — small lateral hinges between each segment angling slightly with soft servo whirrs to form the coil, the tail tip rising up off the desk as the final segments lock into the curl. Individual overlapping scale panels rotate flush along the sides of the now-coiled tail body from beneath flush-fitted slots, each one snapping into position with tiny crisp pings. A thin silver chrome dorsal fin ridge unfolds along the top of the tail from a hidden seam, snapping into a serrated row with a series of tiny spring snaps. The ears flick back along the goat's skull from small recessed slots behind the horn bases with two tiny spring snaps. A final pneumatic hiss as the goat head settles into its proud raised position, the front legs brace wider with a subtle click, and the deep-sapphire gemstone in the forehead settles flush as a small panel above it closes with a final crisp click. During the transformation, the audio is entirely ASMR mechanical sounds — NO music, NO background song, NO narration.
