## How to adapt Mechanical Toys prompts:

## First Frame Image
A smartphone photo taken from an elevated perspective — roughly 45 to 60 degrees above the desk, as if a person standing or sitting is looking down at the toy on the table in front of them. This angle shows both the top surface and the front/side of the toy simultaneously, revealing its three-dimensional shape and surface details. The desk surface takes up most of the frame — at least 70% of the image is the desk stretching out ahead, with only a thin sliver of room visible at the very top edge of the frame. The desk is a worn white or light-colored surface — mostly empty, with subtle signs of real use like faint scratches, a couple of small scuff marks, maybe a tiny chip in the paint near an edge. A small collectible toy figure sits on the desk in its compact folded-up form — a closed, simple geometric shape — a smooth capsule lying flat on its side — with all limbs and parts retracted and tucked in. The compact form rests on the desk in the exact orientation it will transform from — its long axis and footprint already aligned to the final deployed form's footprint, so the transformation can unfold in place without the toy needing to tip, roll, or reposition itself. The shape looks like a mysterious abstract object that could transform into anything. A small round metallic silver button is clearly visible on the top center of the toy — this is the transformation trigger. The toy has a highly detailed surface — a deep midnight purple base with a heavy silver flake clearcoat, dark charcoal matte secondary panels, and polished chrome mechanical trim. Segmented panels and fine mechanical joint lines are visible on the surface suggesting it can transform, but giving no indication of what it becomes. Natural indoor lighting casting a soft shadow beneath and slightly behind the toy. Taken with a phone camera, shallow depth of field — the toy is sharp, the far edge of the desk softly blurs. No text, no watermarks, no logos, no overlays of any kind. Photorealistic.

## End Frame Image
A smartphone photo taken from an elevated perspective — roughly 45 to 60 degrees above the desk, as if a person standing or sitting is looking down at the toy on the table in front of them. This angle shows both the top surface and the front/side of the toy simultaneously, revealing its three-dimensional shape, pose, and surface details from a natural human viewpoint. The desk surface takes up most of the frame — at least 70% of the image is the desk stretching out ahead, with only a thin sliver of room visible at the very top edge of the frame. The desk is a worn white or light-colored surface — mostly empty, with subtle signs of real use like faint scratches, a couple of small scuff marks, maybe a tiny chip in the paint near an edge. The same collectible Spiked Ankylosaurus toy figure is now fully transformed and deployed, sitting on the desk in its complete Ankylosaurus shape with all limbs, spikes, and parts extended and articulated. The toy occupies the same footprint and orientation on the desk as it did in its compact form — it has expanded outward in place, not relocated. It rests in a low, heavy, aggressive stance, with four stout armored legs planted firmly on the desk, its spiked carapace flared slightly upward, and its thick tail swept slightly to one side with a massive mace club resting on the surface. The elevated angle reveals the full three-dimensional detail of the deployed form — the height of raised parts, the depth of recessed panels, the spread of extended limbs. There is no button visible anywhere on the toy — the transformation is complete and the button is now hidden beneath the deployed panels. The toy has a highly detailed surface — a deep midnight purple base with a heavy silver flake clearcoat, dark charcoal matte secondary panels, and polished chrome mechanical trim. Mechanical joints, ball sockets, and segmented armor panels are clearly visible from this angle showing the engineering detail. Natural indoor lighting casting a soft shadow beneath and slightly behind the toy. Taken with a phone camera, shallow depth of field — the toy is sharp, the far edge of the desk softly blurs. No text, no watermarks, no logos, no overlays of any kind. Photorealistic.

## Transition Prompt
A smartphone video filmed handheld with one hand from an elevated angle — roughly 45 to 60 degrees above the desk, looking down and slightly forward, as if a person is standing over the table filming with one hand while the other hand interacts with the toy. The desk surface fills most of the frame. The camera has natural human imperfections — subtle shaky movements, slight tilts, minor focus hunting, small overcorrections as the person adjusts their grip on the phone. The footage feels completely authentic, like someone excitedly recording with one hand on their phone while using their free hand to interact with the toy. The video opens on the toy as shown in the start frame, resting on the desk in its compact form. The camera drifts slightly then corrects — natural handheld sway. A hand enters the frame from the bottom edge — the person's free hand reaching over the desk. The camera jiggles slightly as their body shifts to reach forward. The hand picks up the compact toy carefully, lifting it just an inch or two off the desk while keeping it in the same orientation it was resting in — no flipping, no rotating, no tilting. The thumb finds the silver button on top and presses it down with a satisfying tactile click. A brief pause — nothing happens yet. The hand sets the toy back down in the EXACT same spot and EXACT same orientation it picked it up from, then pulls away out of frame. The camera sways gently as the person shifts their weight back. The toy sits motionless on the desk for a half-second beat of anticipation. A faint internal whirring begins — barely audible, like tiny gears engaging. The transformation begins. The capsule's top surface splits right down the center seam with a sharp metallic snap. The two heavy halves slide outward on lateral mechanical rails to form the wide, heavily armored dorsal carapace. The front dome section of the capsule hinges forward on a stiff underlying pivot, dropping down and locking with a heavy thunk to reveal the mechanical skull. Two small chrome horns flick out from recessed slots on the sides of the head with tiny spring snaps. Four thick legs telescope out from concealed rectangular compartments along the newly exposed underbelly. They deploy in sequence—front pair, then rear pair—swinging down on heavy shoulder hinges and locking into place, planting their flat mechanical feet onto the desk with solid metallic taps. The rounded rear cap of the capsule pushes outward and extends backward with a loud ratcheting tick-tick-tick sound, elongating segment by segment into a thick tail. At the very tip of the tail, two curved panels spring open like a clamshell on hidden hinges, slamming backward and locking together to form the heavy mace club with a deep, resonant thunk. Finally, a series of lateral defense spikes pop out along the outer edges of the carapace with a rapid sequence of crisp metallic pings. During the transformation, the audio is entirely ASMR mechanical sounds — NO music, NO background song, NO narration. The sounds include: precise metallic clicks and snaps as panels lock, soft whirring of tiny servos and gears, ratcheting sounds as segments extend, light tapping as parts contact the desk surface, subtle pneumatic hisses as panels slide, the satisfying thunk of parts locking into final position. Each sound corresponds exactly to the physical movement happening on screen. The overall sound profile is similar to a high-end Transformer toy — tactile, crisp, mechanical, deeply satisfying. The camera reacts naturally throughout the transformation — the person filming flinches slightly when the toy first moves, then leans in closer as the transformation progresses, makes a small adjustment when a part extends toward the camera, and settles into a more stable but still handheld framing as the transformation completes. The focus occasionally hunts briefly when the toy changes shape rapidly, then locks back on. The camera stays at the elevated 45-60 degree angle throughout. The transformation completes, matching the end frame exactly. The camera holds with natural micro-movements and breathing sway. The person slowly shifts the camera angle slightly to show off the final form from a different perspective, then settles back. The last sound is a final definitive click or lock as the last piece settles, then silence except for ambient room tone. Throughout the entire video: No text, no watermarks, no logos, no overlays, no music, no narration. Only mechanical ASMR sounds and subtle ambient room noise. The footage must feel 100% like a real person filmed this on their phone — not a produced studio shoot.
