## First Frame Image
A smartphone photo taken from an elevated perspective — roughly 45 to 60 degrees above the desk, as if a person standing or sitting is looking down at the toy on the table in front of them. This angle shows both the top surface and the front/side of the toy simultaneously, revealing its three-dimensional shape and surface details. The desk surface takes up most of the frame — at least 70% of the image is the desk stretching out ahead, with only a thin sliver of room visible at the very top edge of the frame. The desk is a worn white or light-colored surface — mostly empty, with subtle signs of real use like faint scratches, a couple of small scuff marks, maybe a tiny chip in the paint near an edge. A small collectible toy figure sits on the desk in its compact folded-up form — a closed, simple geometric shape — a smooth horizontal capsule lying on its side, roughly the size of a large vitamin pill, with rounded ends and a perfectly seamless cylindrical body — with all limbs and parts retracted and tucked in. The compact form rests on the desk in the exact orientation it will transform from — its long axis and footprint already aligned to the final deployed beetle's footprint, so the transformation can unfold in place without the toy needing to tip, roll, or reposition itself. The shape looks like a mysterious abstract object that could transform into anything. A small round metallic silver button is clearly visible on the top center of the toy — this is the transformation trigger. The toy has a highly detailed surface — a deep iridescent teal-green metallic finish that shifts to bronze-gold at certain angles, with darker navy-blue accent bands wrapping around the upper and lower thirds of the capsule, brushed brass rivet heads inset along faint horizontal seams, polished silver chrome trim rings at each rounded end cap, fine engraved hairline panel lines suggesting where shell halves and leg compartments meet, a barely-visible sculpted insignia of a small geometric scarab glyph in relief on one side, and a subtle metallic flake clearcoat that catches the light in tiny pinpricks. Segmented panels and fine mechanical joint lines are visible on the surface suggesting it can transform, but giving no indication of what it becomes. Natural indoor lighting casting a soft shadow beneath and slightly behind the toy. Taken with a phone camera, shallow depth of field — the toy is sharp, the far edge of the desk softly blurs. No text, no watermarks, no logos, no overlays of any kind. Photorealistic.

## Transition Prompt
A smartphone video filmed handheld with one hand from an elevated angle — roughly 45 to 60 degrees above the desk, looking down and slightly forward, as if a person is standing over the table filming with one hand while the other hand interacts with the toy. The desk surface fills most of the frame. The camera has natural human imperfections — subtle shaky movements, slight tilts, minor focus hunting, small overcorrections as the person adjusts their grip on the phone. The footage feels completely authentic, like someone excitedly recording with one hand on their phone while using their free hand to interact with the toy. The video opens on the toy as shown in the start frame, resting on the desk in its compact capsule form. The camera drifts slightly then corrects — natural handheld sway. A hand enters the frame from the bottom edge — the person's free hand reaching over the desk. The camera jiggles slightly as their body shifts to reach forward. The hand picks up the compact toy carefully, lifting it just an inch or two off the desk while keeping it in the same horizontal orientation it was resting in — no flipping, no rotating, no tilting. The thumb finds the silver button on top and presses it down with a satisfying tactile click. A brief pause — nothing happens yet. The hand sets the toy back down in the EXACT same spot and EXACT same orientation it picked it up from, then pulls away out of frame. The camera sways gently as the person shifts their weight back. The toy sits motionless on the desk for a half-second beat of anticipation. A faint internal whirring begins — barely audible, like tiny gears engaging. CRITICAL — TRANSFORMATION MUST BE 100% MECHANICAL. NO morphing. NO fading. NO dissolving. NO crossfades. NO parts appearing or disappearing magically. NO smooth shape-blending. Every visible change must be a discrete physical movement: a panel hinging open, a segment sliding out from inside another, a leg unfolding from a recessed compartment, a part rotating on a visible pivot. Every feature in the final deployed form must trace back to a specific seam, panel, hinge, or compartment that was visible on the compact form — nothing materializes from nowhere. The toy stays in place on the desk throughout — it expands outward into its final form within its existing footprint. It does not jump, lift, roll, or reorient itself. The transformation begins. The two chrome end caps of the capsule rotate 90 degrees with a soft servo whirr and retract slightly inward, revealing recessed compartments at the front and rear. From the front cap compartment, the head section telescopes forward on a central rail with a ratcheting tick-tick-tick, then locks with a deep satisfying thunk. The mandibles split apart along a fine front seam on the head and swing outward on twin hinges with sharp metallic clicks, serrations rotating into final spread position. Two fine antennae unfold from tiny slots above the head, each segment extending in three articulated stages with crisp metallic pings. The main capsule shell splits along its visible horizontal centerline seam — the upper half lifts on rear elytra hinges with a pneumatic hiss, dividing further down its dorsal midline into two wing covers that hinge outward on side pivots and lock at a raised display angle with a sharp click. From beneath the lifted wing covers, the translucent inner flight wings fan out halfway on tiny pivot points with a subtle hydraulic sigh, veined membranes catching the light. The lower half of the capsule shell opens its underside — six leg compartments along the ventral seams hinge open in sequence, and the legs deploy in pairs from front to back: each leg telescopes out from its recessed slot in three articulated segments — first the upper segment swinging outward on its shoulder hinge with a soft servo whirr, then the middle segment ratcheting down with a tick-tick-tick, then the lower clawed tip snapping down to the desk surface with a tiny metallic tap. The rear cap compartment retracts fully into the body with a final pneumatic hiss as the abdomen segments lock into shape. The compound eyes on the head sides flip into place from tiny rotating sockets with twin crisp pings. During the transformation, the audio is entirely ASMR mechanical sounds — NO music, NO background song, NO narration. The sounds include: precise metallic clicks and snaps as panels lock, soft whirring of tiny servos and gears, ratcheting sounds as segments extend, light tapping as legs contact the desk surface, subtle pneumatic hisses as panels slide, the satisfying thunk of parts locking into final position. Each sound corresponds exactly to the physical movement happening on screen. The overall sound profile is similar to a high-end Transformer toy — tactile, crisp, mechanical, deeply satisfying. The camera reacts naturally throughout the transformation — the person filming flinches slightly when the toy first moves, then leans in closer as the transformation progresses, makes a small adjustment when the wing covers extend toward the camera, and settles into a more stable but still handheld framing as the transformation completes. The focus occasionally hunts briefly when the toy changes shape rapidly, then locks back on. The camera stays at the elevated 45-60 degree angle throughout. The transformation completes, matching the end frame exactly. The camera holds with natural micro-movements and breathing sway. The person slowly shifts the camera angle slightly to show off the final beetle form from a different perspective, then settles back. The last sound is a final definitive click as the antennae lock into their final curve, then silence except for ambient room tone. Throughout the entire video: No text, no watermarks, no logos, no overlays, no music, no narration. Only mechanical ASMR sounds and subtle ambient room noise. The footage must feel 100% like a real person filmed this on their phone — not a produced studio shoot.
